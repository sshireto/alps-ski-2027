Ischgl

10–16 Jan
For intermediate skiers
Red-run cruising. With 140 km red versus 47 km blue, this is the strongest red-heavy choice for confident intermediates. The mileage spans Austria and Switzerland.
Snow & altitude
High terrain and extensive snowmaking (95% coverage listed by Skiresort). No glacier skiing. This supports reliability, not a guarantee that every run will open.
Après-ski & the return home
The party focus is Ischgl, including Trofana Alm and Kuhstall; Samnaun is quieter. NordOst is a village base, so plan the walk back rather than expecting piste-side accommodation.
From the booked stay
· €138.97 pp/night. Mapped walk: 550 m / about 8 minutes to Pardatschgratbahn; the hotel advertises about 6 minutes. No verified direct ski-in/out.
Getting there
Rail to Landeck-Zams, then bus 260 into the valley. The official site also lists Zurich and Munich airport transfer options.
Sources, scope & counting notes
  • This is the connected cross-border Silvretta Arena, not all separate resorts on a wider Silvretta pass.
  • Skiresort reports 1,360–2,872 m; Ischgl tourism reports 1,377–2,872 m. The operator gives Pardatschgratbahn's valley station as 1,365 m, showing why village and lowest lift elevations differ. Greitspitze has ordinary pistes from the summit.
  • Official Ischgl page lists 239 km total but 47 blue + 140 red + 47 black, which sums to 234. Use Skiresort's 47 / 140 / 52 = 239 breakdown rather than silently correcting the official page.
  • Official Ischgl tourism lists 46 lifts; Skiresort lists 45. If displaying lifts, keep the provider label visible.

St. Anton

10–16 Jan
For intermediate skiers
The broadest terrain choice. The 250 km blue/red figure covers the whole Ski Arlberg domain, including Lech/Zürs and Warth/Schröcken. Plan sectors and return connections; it is not all immediately above St. Anton.
Snow & altitude
No glacier skiing. Snowmaking supplements natural snow, but the wider domain contains different elevations and exposures. Fresh conditions and connections need checking close to travel.
Après-ski & the return home
St. Anton drives the domain’s lively reputation, including Mooser Wirt and Krazy Kanguruh. Parseierblick is at Nasserein, so the last trip home from central or mountain venues still needs planning.
From the booked stay
· €144.05 pp/night, breakfast included. Across from Nassereinbahn: the mapped walking route is 72 m / about 1 minute. A final road crossing remains; exact doorstep ski-in is not confirmed.
Getting there
St. Anton has a mainline station in the village. The regional arrival guide lists Zurich airport transfers; January 2027 services and timings must be checked separately.
Sources, scope & counting notes
  • Whole Ski Arlberg: St. Anton–St. Christoph–Stuben, Lech–Oberlech–Zürs and Warth–Schröcken. The large powder/off-piste offering is excluded.
  • 2,811 m is the lift-served Valluga summit. The Tirol tourism board states that skiing from Valluga is restricted to proficient skiers accompanied by a mountain or ski guide. Do not imply a normal groomed piste starts at the summit.
  • Official Ski Arlberg overview currently advertises 301 km; Skiresort displays 300 km with category sum 299.7 km. Comparison uses Skiresort consistently.
  • The tourism board's older Winter 2024/25 press pack gives 129 blue / 121 red / 50 black. Do not mix this older breakdown with the current comparison.

Sölden

10–16 Jan
For intermediate skiers
A varied intermediate week. Giggijoch and glacier sectors offer wider easier runs; Gaislachkogl has more intermediate terrain. The blue/red total is 116.5 km.
Snow & altitude
The only glacier option here: Rettenbach and Tiefenbach, with skiing above 3,000 m. Skiresort lists 91% snowmaking coverage. Wind, weather and open links still matter.
Après-ski & the return home
Mountain bars and town venues stretch between Giggijochbahn and Gaislachkoglbahn. Valentin is near Gaislachkogl, so check the return distance when choosing an evening venue farther along the village.
From the booked stay
· €149.35 pp/night. The hotel advertises 150–200 m to Gaislachkoglbahn, while the mapped street walk is 350 m / about 5 minutes. The separate Hotel Valentin is not this booking.
Getting there
Official driving estimates: Munich airport 3 h 15 min; Zurich airport 3 h 30 min. These are planning estimates, not live traffic predictions or a booked transfer.
Sources, scope & counting notes
  • Sölden’s connected winter area, including both glacier sectors. The wider Ötztal ski-pass portfolio is not added.
  • Use 1,350–3,250 m for lift-served skiing. The advertised 3,340 m maximum includes Schwarze Schneid's viewing platform, reached by a 15-minute walk from the gondola. Keep village elevation separate.
  • Official overview advertises 147 km; official pages contain inconsistent older breakdowns. Skiresort states 145.7 km plus 8.4 km routes; its slope page rounds the total to 146 but the three grades sum to 145.7. Use 145.7 consistently and disclose source/counting differences.

Obertauern

24–30 Jan
For intermediate skiers
The easiest grade mix for relaxed cruising: 61 km blue plus 35 km red, or 96% of the area. The Tauernrunde circuit is compact, with less overall mileage than the other three.
Snow & altitude
The highest village of these four at 1,740 m, with a ski-area base at 1,630 m. No glacier; Skiresort lists 95% snowmaking coverage. High altitude is helpful, not a trip-date snow forecast.
Après-ski & the return home
Edelweissalm and Hochalm are well-known afternoon stops, with bars in the centre later. DIE SONNE’s shuttle ends at 17:00, so arrange the late return separately.
From the booked stay
· €134.77 pp/night for five; shuttle 09:00–17:00 or a mapped 550 m / 8-minute lift walk. · €165.67 for six, breakfast included; short walk to bridge 5 (about 50 m in a secondary listing), then ski onward; return almost to the door.
Getting there
Solaria lists Munich airport at about 250 km. Rail access is via Radstadt, about 21 km away, followed by bus or a pre-arranged road transfer.
Sources, scope & counting notes
  • Obertauern only: 100 connected piste kilometres. Grosseck-Speiereck is a separate area roughly 15 km away even where included on the same pass; it is excluded here.
  • 1,740–2,526 m refers to village/mountain geography and is not the lift-served ski range. The official winter and piste-map pages give 1,630–2,313 m.
  • Obertauern passes of 1.5 days or longer also cover Grosseck-Speiereck, around 15 km away. This is a separate outing, not extra connected Obertauern mileage. SuperSkiCard and LUNGO are broader pass products and should not inflate the 100 km comparison.
